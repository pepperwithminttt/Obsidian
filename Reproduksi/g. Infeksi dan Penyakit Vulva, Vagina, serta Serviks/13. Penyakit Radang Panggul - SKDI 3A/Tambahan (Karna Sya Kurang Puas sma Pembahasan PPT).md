Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) is an **infection and inflammation of the upper female reproductive tract**—including the uterus, fallopian tubes, and ovaries—usually **caused by bacteria spreading upward from the vagina or cervix**.

# Causes

- **Sexually transmitted infections (STIs):** Most commonly caused by _Chlamydia trachomatis_ and _Neisseria gonorrhoeae_.
- **Vaginal flora imbalances:** Overgrowth of normal bacteria, such as those linked to bacterial vaginosis.
- **Medical procedures:** Rarely introduced during intrauterine device (IUD) insertion or other procedures that enter the uterus.

# Symptoms

- Pain in the lower belly or pelvis
- Unusual or heavy vaginal discharge with a strong odor
- Pain or burning during urination
- Pain during sexual intercourse (dyspareunia)
- Bleeding between menstrual periods
- Fever or chills (in more severe cases)
- _Note:_ Some individuals experience very mild symptoms or no symptoms at all.

# Treatment

- **Antibiotics:** A standard 14-day course of oral or injectable antibiotics to clear the bacterial infection.
- **Partner treatment:** Sexual partners must also be evaluated and treated to prevent reinfection.
- **Hospitalization:** Required for severe cases, abscesses, or if the patient is pregnant.

# Complications

Untreated PID can cause permanent damage to the reproductive system, leading to:

- Chronic (long-term) pelvic pain
- Infertility (difficulty getting pregnant)
- Ectopic pregnancy (pregnancy outside the womb)
- Tubo-ovarian abscesses (pockets of infected fluid/pus)
